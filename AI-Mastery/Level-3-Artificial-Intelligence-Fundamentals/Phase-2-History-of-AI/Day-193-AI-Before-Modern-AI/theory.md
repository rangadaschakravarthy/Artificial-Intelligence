# Day 193 Theory: AI Before Modern AI & Early Ideas

### 1. What Is It?
The history of AI prior to digital electronic computing encompasses centuries of philosophical inquiry, formal mathematical logic, and mechanical automata attempting to mechanize human thought.

### 2. Key Historical Milestones
- **Aristotelian Syllogism (350 BCE)**: First formal system of deductive logic ("All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; Therefore Socrates is mortal").
- **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1666)**: Envisioned the *Calculemus* ("Let us calculate")—a universal formal language (*Characteristica Universalis*) resolving human disputes via mathematical calculation.
- **George Boole (1854)**: Published *The Laws of Thought*, establishing Boolean Algebra mapping binary truth values ($1$ and $0$) to logical operations ($\text{AND}, \text{OR}, \text{NOT}$).
- **Ada Lovelace (1843)**: Wrote the first algorithm for Babbage's Analytical Engine, correctly predicting that mechanical computation could manipulate general symbols beyond mere numbers.

### 3. Summary
AI roots stem from formal logic, Boolean algebra, and mechanical computation enabling general symbol processing.

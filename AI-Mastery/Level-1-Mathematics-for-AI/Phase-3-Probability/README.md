# Phase 3 — Probability for Artificial Intelligence

## Overview
Probability theory is the mathematical framework for handling uncertainty, noise, and incomplete information in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning. From probabilistic classifiers (Naive Bayes) to probabilistic generative models (VAEs, Diffusion Models) and reinforcement learning policies, probability enables AI systems to make optimal decisions, quantify confidence, and model random phenomena.

## Concepts Covered
- Random Experiments, Sample Spaces, Events, and Axioms of Probability
- Addition Rule, Multiplication Rule, Complement, and Mutually Exclusive Events
- Conditional Probability $P(A|B)$ and Information Updating
- Bayes' Theorem, Prior, Likelihood, Posterior, and Marginal Likelihood
- Discrete and Continuous Random Variables, PMF, PDF, and CDF
- Common Distributions: Bernoulli, Binomial, Uniform, Gaussian (Normal), Poisson
- Mathematical Expectation $E[X]$, Variance $Var(X)$, Standard Deviation
- Joint, Marginal, and Conditional Distributions
- Covariance $Cov(X,Y)$, Pearson Correlation $\rho_{X,Y}$
- Independence, Conditional Independence, and Naive Bayes Assumption
- Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Foundations
- Probabilistic ML Models (Naive Bayes Classifier) & Python Implementations

## AI Connections
- **Bayes' Theorem**: Underpins Bayesian Inference, Naive Bayes Classification, and Spam Filtering.
- **Expectation & Variance**: Used in Loss Functions, Risk Minimization, policy gradients in Reinforcement Learning, and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs).
- **Probability Distributions**: Foundation for Noise Modeling, Softmax output activation interpreting logits as probabilities, and Diffusion Models.
- **Joint & Marginal Distributions**: Crucial for Probabilistic Graphical Models, Attention Mechanism probability weightings, and Cross-Entropy Loss.

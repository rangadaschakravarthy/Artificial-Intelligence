# Day 200 Theory: First AI Winter (1974–1980)

### 1. What Is It?
The First AI Winter was a major slump in AI funding, commercial interest, and academic research lasting from 1974 to 1980, triggered by over-hyped expectations failing to meet real-world results.

### 2. Core Catalyst Factors
1. **Combinatorial Explosion (Lighthill Report 1973)**: Search spaces grow exponentially $O(b^d)$. Early search algorithms scaled hopelessly beyond toy microworlds.
2. **The XOR Problem (*Perceptrons* 1969)**: Minsky and Papert proved that single-layer perceptrons could not solve linearly non-separable logic gates like XOR ($\text{Exclusive-OR}$), halting neural network funding for a decade.
3. **Hardware Constraints**: 1970s mainframes had under 1 MB RAM and megahertz processing speeds.

### 3. Summary
The First AI Winter was caused by combinatorial explosion, the XOR single-layer perceptron limit, and severe 1970s hardware constraints.
